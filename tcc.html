Try · Confirm · Cancel

TCC splits every step into a reservation and a later decision. Each service first tries (freezes money, reserves stock, locks a coupon), then the coordinator tells all of them to confirm or cancel. Unlike 2PC there are no database locks held in between, only business-level reservations, so the hard parts move into the participants.

    Terms used here

    Try
    Check the business rules and reserve the resource without using it: freeze money, mark stock as reserved, lock the coupon. Nothing is visible to the customer yet.
    Confirm
    Turn the reservation into the real change. It must not fail for business reasons (Try already checked) and must be idempotent, because the coordinator retries it until it gets a reply.
    Cancel
    Release the reservation. Also retried until it succeeds, so it must be idempotent too.
    Empty cancel (null compensation)
    A Cancel for a transaction whose Try never arrived. The participant must answer OK and record a "cancelled" marker instead of failing.
    Suspension (hanging Try)
    A delayed Try that arrives after its Cancel. Without a check against the cancel marker it reserves resources that nobody will ever confirm or cancel.
    TCC vs 2PC
    2PC holds database locks across the protocol. TCC holds business reservations instead, so other work can continue, but every service has to implement three operations and their edge cases.
    TCC vs saga
    A saga commits each step immediately and undoes it later with a compensation, so others can briefly see the change. TCC never makes the change visible until Confirm.
