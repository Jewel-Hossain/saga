Integration patterns

Three classic enterprise integration patterns for shaping where messages go and what they carry: ask many and combine the answers (scatter-gather), let the message carry its own route (routing slip), and keep big payloads out of the broker (claim check).

    Terms used here

    Scatter-gather
    Broadcast one request to several recipients, then use an aggregator to collect the replies into one answer.
    Aggregator · completion condition
    Groups replies by correlation id and decides when the group is done: all replies in, a fixed timeout, or "first good enough". Late replies to a closed group are discarded.
    Routing slip
    The sender attaches the list of processing steps to the message. Each step does its work, removes itself from the list and forwards to the next address. Steps don't need to know about each other.
    Routing slip vs process manager
    A routing slip fixes the route when the message is created. If the route depends on results along the way, a process manager (or saga orchestrator) is the better fit.
    Claim check
    Store a large payload somewhere else (object storage, a database), send only a reference, and let the receiver fetch it. Brokers stay fast and messages stay under size limits.
    Message size limits
    Kafka rejects records over message.max.bytes (about 1 MB by default); RabbitMQ has max_message_size. Even below the limit, large messages hurt memory, replication and latency for everyone.
