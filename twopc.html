Two-phase commit

One checkout touches three databases. A coordinator asks every participant to prepare, then tells all of them to commit or abort. Crash things at different points and see who is left holding locks, and for how long.

    Terms used here

    Coordinator · participant
    The coordinator (transaction manager) runs the protocol and owns the decision. Participants (resource managers) are the databases doing the work.
    Phase 1: PREPARE / vote
    Each participant makes its changes durable without making them visible, keeps its locks, and votes YES. A YES is a promise to commit if told to, even after a crash.
    Phase 2: COMMIT / ABORT
    If every vote is YES the coordinator decides COMMIT, otherwise ABORT, and tells every prepared participant. Participants ACK so the coordinator can forget the transaction.
    Commit point
    The moment the coordinator's decision record reaches disk. Before it, the transaction can still abort; after it, it will commit whatever crashes next.
    In doubt
    A participant that has voted YES but not heard the decision. It can't commit (someone else may have voted NO) and can't abort (the coordinator may have decided COMMIT).
    Blocking
    While participants are in doubt they keep their locks, so other transactions on those rows wait until the coordinator comes back. This is 2PC's main weakness.
    Presumed abort
    If the coordinator's log has no decision for a transaction, it is treated as aborted. That's what lets a recovering coordinator answer in-doubt participants.
    Heuristic decision
    Committing or rolling back an in-doubt transaction by hand (COMMIT PREPARED / ROLLBACK PREPARED) to free locks. If the guess disagrees with the coordinator, atomicity is lost.
    XA
    The standard interface for 2PC between a transaction manager and resource managers. Every participant has to support prepare, so a card network or HTTP API can't take part. That's one reason sagas exist.
